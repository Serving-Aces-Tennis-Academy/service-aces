Why I Don't Start Beginners on a Western Grip

By Coach Tommy O'Donnell November 23, 2026 4 min read

Here's something I see a lot.

A new player goes to their first lesson somewhere. Before they've hit ten balls, they're holding the racquet in a semi-western or western forehand grip. It doesn't matter if they're 6 or 60. It doesn't matter if they've never played. A lot of coaches start everybody there.

I think that's a mistake. Here's why, and what I do instead.

First, what a grip actually is

The handle of a racquet has eight flat sides, called bevels. Your grip is just which bevel the big knuckle of your index finger sits on.

Turn your hand a little and you have a different grip. That small turn changes the angle of the strings when you hit. And that changes how the ball comes off the racquet.

Why the western grips are popular

To be fair, there's a reason so many coaches teach them.

The semi-western is the most common forehand grip on the pro tour today. It makes it easier to hit heavy topspin and to handle balls that bounce up high. For an advanced player with a fast swing, it works.

But here's the problem. A beginner isn't an advanced player.

What goes wrong when you start there

Low balls get hard. With your hand under the handle, the strings face down toward the court. To lift a low ball over the net, you need a big, fast, low-to-high swing. New players don't have that swing yet, so the ball goes in the net. Over and over.

Every other shot needs a different grip. You can't serve, volley, or hit an overhead well with a western grip. Those shots all use the continental grip. So a player who only knows the western has to learn a second grip from scratch, and the old habit fights them every time.

It's hard to undo. Once your hand learns a grip, it goes back there under pressure. Changing it later is a lot more work than starting in the right place.

Why I start with the continental

The continental grip is the one you use for the serve, the volley, the overhead, and the slice. It's the base the rest of the game is built on. Learn it first, and half your shots already have the right grip.

It's also simple to find. Hold the racquet out in front of you with the edge of the frame pointing down, like you're about to tap a nail with a hammer. Now grab the handle the way you'd grab a hammer. That's it. The big knuckle of your index finger should be on bevel 2.

That's where I start my students, kids included.

As a player gets better, their forehand grip can turn a little toward an eastern or semi-western if their game calls for it. That's a choice you make later, on purpose, once the basics are there. Not on day one.

Ace’s Tip

Not sure what grip you're using? Hold your racquet the way you normally would, then look at the big knuckle of your index finger. Find the flat side of the handle it's resting on, and check it against the picture above.

If you already learned a western grip

You're not doomed. Plenty of good players hit with a western forehand.

But you'll still need a continental for your serve, volleys, and overheads. If those shots feel awkward, your grip is the first thing I'd check.

And if you're just starting out, or you're signing up your kid, ask what grip they'll be taught first. It's a fair question. It tells you a lot about how a coach thinks.
